Multifamily and HOA — where the roof is the easy part.

What a board actually needs

  • A written assessment they can circulate before a vote
  • Budget numbers that survive a reserve study conversation
  • A schedule residents can be told about in advance
  • Someone who will attend the meeting and answer questions

What property managers get

  • One contact, not a rotating crew lead
  • Notice given to residents before work starts
  • Parking, access and debris planned, not improvised
  • Paperwork closed out the same week we finish
Most roofing companies treat a hundred-unit HOA like a hundred small jobs. It is one job with a hundred people watching, and the difference is entirely in the communication.
