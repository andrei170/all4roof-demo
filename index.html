Commercial roofing.

On a commercial building the roof is rarely the hard part. Getting a decision made, funded and approved is the hard part — so that is where we start.

Assessment first

A written property condition assessment with photographs, measurements and remaining service life.

  • Budget figures you can take to an owner or board
  • An honest answer on repair versus replacement
  • Something that survives a reserve study conversation

Replacement

Flat, low-slope, TPO, modified and shingle systems, phased to keep the building operating.

  • Manufacturer warranty documentation handled
  • One point of contact from survey to sign-off
  • Work sequenced around tenants, not through them

Storm & insurance

Colorado hail documented to an insurer's standard, with the scope written so nothing gets missed.

  • We meet the adjuster on site
  • Scope written before the claim is filed
  • A straight answer on whether to claim at all

Multifamily & HOA

Where the roof is the easy part.

A hundred-unit community is not a hundred small jobs. It is one job with a hundred people watching, and the difference is entirely in the communication.

What a board needs

Something they can circulate before a vote

  • A written assessment, not a verbal number
  • Budget figures that hold up against a reserve study
  • A schedule residents can be told about in advance
  • Someone who will attend the meeting and answer questions
What property managers get

One contact, not a rotating crew lead

  • Notice given to residents before work starts
  • Parking, access and debris planned, not improvised
  • Paperwork closed out the same week we finish
  • One number to call when a resident calls you

What boards and property managers ask.

Can you provide a written property condition assessment?

Yes — that is usually where we start on a commercial or multifamily building. Photographs, measurements, remaining service life and budget figures, in a document a board can circulate.

Will someone attend our board meeting?

Yes. Explaining a roof to a board is part of the job, not an extra.

How do you handle residents during the work?

Notice before we start, a schedule they can see, and parking and access planned in advance rather than improvised on the morning.

Do you work with our insurer on hail damage?

Yes. We document the damage to an insurer's standard and meet the adjuster on site, with the scope written before the claim is filed.

Can you give a second opinion on a quote we already have?

Yes, and we will tell you if the quote you are holding is reasonable. Sometimes it is.

What roofing systems do you work with?

Flat and low-slope systems including TPO and modified, as well as conventional shingle on pitched multifamily buildings.
